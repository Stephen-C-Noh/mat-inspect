Five students, one inspection system

Each area of the system has a named owner who built it and answers for it. The split below is not a plan; it is what the ticket history and the code owners file actually show after thirteen weeks.

CN

Changbeom Noh

Delivery, platform, security

Runs the pipeline the rest of the team ships through: repository, CI, containers, the identity setup, and the environment the system actually runs on.

  • Entra sign-in and app roles, proven end to end across five roles
  • Threat model, plus security scanning in CI and an internal penetration test
  • On-premise voice-to-text service
  • Nightly backups and two full restore drills from the handover package
  • Handover package: deployment, security, and runbooks
Second reviewer · compliance paths
EC

Enzo Campana

Inspection domain, audit trail

Owns the rules that decide what an inspection means: what gets asked, what a submission does, and how the record proves it was not altered.

  • Checklist templates and versioning for all four equipment classes
  • Inspection submit with operator attestation
  • Equipment readiness computed at read time, never stored stale
  • Audit delivery through the transactional outbox and hash chain
  • Signed PDF and CSV reports, plus chain verification on startup
FE

Fathema Begum Ema

Operator app

Builds everything the operator touches, on a phone, in a shop, often wearing gloves. This is the half of the system that has to survive real conditions.

  • Sign-in on the phone, redirect-based so it works on iOS
  • QR scan and equipment lookup
  • Voice capture with a transcript the operator reviews before it is kept
  • Photo capture on a failed item
  • The lockout screen an operator sees when equipment is out of service

How the team worked

Ownership you can check, not just claim

Every change carries a ticket

Commits and pull requests lead with a ticket key, so any line of code traces back to the reason it was written and the person who took it on.

Two reviewers where it counts

The audit service, the authentication middleware, the inspection domain, and database migrations each require a second named reviewer before merge.

Eight two-week sprints

Each sprint ended on a demonstrable milestone rather than a status update: a checklist rendering, a failure locking out a machine, a verified audit chain.

Drills, not assurances

The handover package was tested by rebuilding the system from it on a clean machine, twice, rather than being written and filed.
