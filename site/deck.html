MAT‑Inspect

Pre‑use equipment inspection, off paper and onto a phone. Cranes, forklifts, trucks, and a pallet jack: checked, recorded, and locked out when they fail.

10 units  ·  4 overhead cranes · 3 forklifts · 2 trucks · 1 pallet jack
The problem

A paper sheet cannot prove anything

Today

Binders in a shop

Inspection sheets are filled in by hand, filed in a binder, and read only when someone goes looking. A missing page looks exactly like a page that was never filled in.

The risk

Nothing stops the lift

A failed crane can be used again ten minutes later. Nothing connects the failure to the equipment, and no one is told.

The obligation

Records must hold up

Alberta OHS requires a log book of pre‑use inspections. Handwriting on a shelf is a weak answer to an inspector.

Operator, on a phone, before the shift

Five steps, under two minutes

ScanPoint the phone at the QR tag on the machine. The right checklist opens for that class of equipment.
CheckWork the list item by item. Pass or fail, in the order a trained operator already walks the machine.
DescribeSpeak a defect note instead of typing it. Add a photo of what you found.
ConfirmRead back every answer on one screen, then attest that you performed the inspection yourself.
SubmitThe record is written once, stamped with the server's time, and never edited again.
When something fails

The failure moves the machine, not just a form

A blocking failure takes the equipment out of service immediately. A supervisor is notified. Readiness is recomputed on every read, so no stale row can say a locked‑out machine is fine.

Ready · passed today Warning · recorded, still usable Out of service · blocking failure

Equipment returns to service only when a supervisor clears the defect, and only a fresh passing inspection dated the same day makes it ready again.

The constraint that shapes everything

A competent human performs the inspection

Alberta OHS puts the inspection in a trained operator's hands. That is not a feature we chose, and it rules out the obvious shortcuts.

AI is assistive

It listens, it never decides

The AI service turns a spoken defect note into text. No model can pass or fail an inspection. There is no code path where it could.

Privacy

Audio stays on our machines

Voice is biometric information under FOIP. Transcription runs on‑premise, the clip is never sent to an outside service, and the audio is not kept.

Identity

Every record names a person

The operator comes from a validated sign‑in token, never from what the app claims. Anonymous submission is not possible.

How it is built

Two apps, four services, two databases

Why the record can be trusted

Built to fail closed

Immutable

Written once

Inspections, their answers, and audit entries cannot be updated or deleted. The database itself refuses, not just the application.

Hash chain

History that shows its seams

Each audit entry carries the hash of the one before it. Any edit to the past breaks the chain, and the service will not start on a broken chain.

Outbox

The two logs cannot disagree

An inspection and its audit entry are committed in a single transaction, so a service being down at submit time cannot lose the trail.

Auth

A missed check stops the boot

Any route without an authentication handler crashes the service at startup. A forgotten role check becomes a failed deploy, never an open endpoint.

What gets handed over

An artifact, not a promise

Delivered

Delivered
A governance package: runbooks, restore drills, change management, vulnerability handling, and the licence and copyright terms.
Demonstrated
The same images running on managed cloud infrastructure with synthetic data, to prove that path works before anyone commits to it. Torn down at handover.
Not in scope
A campus‑hosted production deployment. SAIT ITS declined to host during the capstone, so the system is built to be self‑hosted or moved to campus infrastructure later without redesign.
